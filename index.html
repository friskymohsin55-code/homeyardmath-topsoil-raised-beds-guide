How Much Topsoil Do You Need for a Raised Bed?

By Syed Mohsin Ali — November 18, 2026

Ordering topsoil is a volume problem, not a guessing game. The formula is simple: Cubic Yards = (Length × Width × (Depth ÷ 12)) ÷ 27. Dry to damp screened topsoil weighs about 2,100 lbs (1.05 tons) per cubic yard. Run the numbers once and you order the right amount the first time.

A real worked example

Take the default job on HomeYardMath's Topsoil Calculator: a 25 ft × 10 ft bed filled 3 inches deep. Area first: 25 × 10 = 250 sq ft. Convert depth to feet: 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 ft. Multiply: 250 × 0.25 = 62.5 cu ft. Divide by 27: 62.5 ÷ 27 = 2.31 cu yd of topsoil. Add the calculator's 10% waste allowance for uneven ground and settling: 2.31 × 1.10 = 2.55 cu yd, or 68.75 cu ft. Weight comes from the density of 2,100 lbs per cu yd: 2.55 × 2,100 = 5,347 lbs (2.67 tons) with waste. After rain the same yard can weigh up to 2,700 lbs, so plan wheelbarrow trips for the wet number — about 6,885 lbs for this job.

Bags or bulk? The price gap is big

Bag math: 68.75 ÷ 0.75 = 91.7, so round up to 92 bags of the 0.75 cu ft size at $2.98 each, or $274.16. The 1.0 cu ft bags need 69 bags at $3.85, or $265.65. Bulk is far cheaper: 2.55 × $35 = $89.25 plus the $80 delivery fee, or $169.25 — a saving of about $105 over bags. Bags run $107.28 per yard against $35 bulk, so the $80 delivery fee pays for itself once the job passes about 1.1 cu yd — roughly 40 bags. Anything bigger than a small patch belongs in bulk. Bags still make sense for a single raised-bed top-up or a small patch.

What to order, and how deep

Buy bulk from a local landscape supply yard and ask for screened topsoil, not fill dirt: screened soil is free of rocks, roots, and clods, and it is the only one meant as a growing layer. For a new lawn or bed, the honest blend is about 70 percent topsoil to 30 percent compost — the topsoil gives structure, the compost gives life. Lawn repairs take 1 to 2 inches per pass so existing grass can grow through; new beds take 6 to 12 inches of growing layer over loosened subsoil. And keep the grade falling away from the house — about 6 inches of drop over the first 10 feet.
